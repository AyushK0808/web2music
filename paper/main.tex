\begin{abstract}
Generative audio models can produce plausible instrumental music from a
text prompt, but placing one inside an always-on consumer application
raises three problems model quality alone does not solve: the prompt must
be derived from something, the derivation must not leak the user's
browsing history, and the model is far too slow for the critical path of a
page load. We present Web2Music, a Chrome extension generating
mood-adaptive ambient music conditioned on the page being read. A
four-signal extraction layer (text, a sentence embedding, page colour,
scroll/cursor behaviour) produces a page descriptor at a median cost of
86 ms across 105 websites; an ablation finds two of the four signals move
nothing measurable. A three-tier classifier (free keyword heuristic, an
abstaining zero-shot NLI tier, a remote LLM as last resort) maps that
descriptor onto mood and category, with two confidence thresholds forming
a continuous dial between accuracy and LLM-specific exposure, though not,
with the hosted backend measured here, over how much text leaves the
device in total. A fallback-then-swap playback design hides a generator
whose median cold generation takes 139 s behind a pre-generated clip
served in 7 ms, while bar-snapped loop detection and a 50 ms crossfade
turn a one-shot generator's output into loopable material. We report a
systems evaluation over 105 websites, 144 generation requests and 141
clips, an adversarial refusal audit, and a cost model showing the cascade
classifies a thousand pages for 0.0132 USD -- alongside four negative
results: the crossfade removes a median of only 0.93 dB of seam
discontinuity, the delivered clip retains a median of 50.3 percent of its
requested duration, the sensitive-content detector misses 65.5 percent of
an adversarial crisis slice, and three annotators agree on the mood label
at only $\alpha=0.195$, rising to just $0.300$ under Russell's
valence-arousal quadrants -- still short of usable, evidence that mood is
underdetermined by page content at any resolution tested.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
% ===========================================================================

Background music is chosen once, for a session, and then ignored: a lo-fi
playlist runs for four hours across a tax form, a horror wiki and a birthday
message without acknowledging that anything changed. It is context-free
because making it context-aware has historically required a human curator or
a library covering every context in advance. Text-conditioned generative
audio models remove that constraint in principle (\S\ref{sec:related}): if
the description can be derived from what the user is doing, the curator is
no longer needed.

Deriving it is where the engineering lives, via three constraints, none
about audio quality. \textbf{The prompt has to come from somewhere:} a page
is a DOM of navigation chrome, ads and colour, not a prompt.
\textbf{Classification is a privacy decision:} the most accurate way to
classify a page is an LLM call, but doing that on every page turns a music
player into a browsing-history exfiltration channel, so the question is how
few pages must be sent to one. \textbf{The model is too slow for the
critical path:} we measure a median of 139 s to generate a 28 s clip against
a page load of about two seconds, a gap no optimisation closes; the
interaction must be designed so it is invisible.

\subsection{Contributions}

First, a four-signal page descriptor over 105 real websites: an ablation
finds category is decided by text alone regardless of what else is
present; behaviour and the embedding move mood on zero pages; colour moves
it on only 7.5 percent.

Second, an abstaining three-tier classifier as a privacy dial (free
keyword heuristic $\to$ zero-shot NLI $\to$ remote LLM), trading accuracy
against \emph{LLM-specific exposure}; with the hosted zero-shot backend
measured here, that dial redistributes disclosure rather than reducing it.

Third, a latency-hiding playback design: instant fallback followed by an
abort-on-stale crossfaded swap, with its full latency distribution and
failure modes; and loop synthesis from a non-loop model, bar-snapped
chroma self-similarity, a 50 ms crossfade, and a gapless Ogg/Opus
container, evaluated over 141 clips.

Finally, an evaluation apparatus against overclaiming: every number is
emitted by a named script against a metric registry that rejects
undeclared metrics and reports blocked artefacts rather than placeholders.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
% ===========================================================================

\subsection{Text-conditioned generation, and affective alternatives}

MusicGen \cite{musicgen} is the generator used here: a single-stage
transformer over discrete EnCodec \cite{encodec} tokens conditioned on
free-text. MusicLM \cite{musiclm} produces higher-fidelity long-form audio
hierarchically; AudioLDM \cite{audioldm} improves quality and speed;
AudioGen \cite{audiogen} applies the same conditioning to environmental
audio. We use the smallest MusicGen variant, the only one producing a 28 s
clip in a time a fallback path can plausibly hide.

A separate line drives music from an explicit affective specification.
AffectMachine-Pop \cite{affectmachine} generates pop music in real time from
a target valence--arousal point, rule-based rather than learned; Web2Music
takes the opposite trade-off. MusicSense \cite{musicsense} recommends
existing tracks from an emotional model of the text being read, the closest
predecessor in intent, but retrieves where we generate (\S\ref{sec:res-retrieval}).
The affective vocabulary derives from Russell's circumplex \cite{russell}
and Thayer's arousal model \cite{thayer}; our problem is not strictly a
classification problem with ground truth (\S\ref{sec:limitations}).
Context-aware computing \cite{schilit, dey} and calm technology
\cite{weiser} supply the framing; the irrelevant-speech effect
\cite{salame} motivates instrumental, non-lyrical material.

\subsection{Zero-shot classification, cascades, and page signal}

The classifier's middle tier follows the NLI formulation of zero-shot
classification \cite{zeroshotnli}: a label becomes a hypothesis scored by an
entailment model, admitting an unseen label set without task-specific
training. Generative models offer an alternative route \cite{zeroshotgen} —
what our remote tier provides — over sentence embeddings \cite{sbert} small
enough for in-browser inference. We exploit \emph{abstention}: entailment
cannot invent a label outside its set, unlike a generative tier. Cascading
cheap classifiers ahead of expensive ones is established, Viola--Jones
\cite{violajones} to LLM cost-routing \cite{frugalgpt}; our contribution is
that on a user's machine, the cascade controls disclosure, not cost.
Boilerplate removal \cite{boilerplate}, readability \cite{flesch},
dominant-colour extraction \cite{dominantcolour} and cursor/scroll
engagement signals \cite{arapakis, cursorcikm} motivate our four signals.

\subsection{Loop synthesis}

Self-similarity \cite{foote} gives candidate loop points, beat tracking
\cite{librosa} the bar grid, loudness the broadcast standard \cite{ebu128},
and the clip is carried in Opus \cite{opus}, whose container semantics,
unlike MP3's, permit genuinely gapless playback.

\section{Research Gaps and Problem Statement}
\label{sec:gaps}
% ===========================================================================

\subsection{Gaps}

Reading the two bodies of work above against each other exposes four gaps.
\textbf{Deployment-level properties of generative systems go unmeasured:}
how a prompt is obtained, what it costs, what happens during generation,
and whether any safeguard survives adversarial or near-miss input all fall
outside the audio-quality frame prior work uses. \textbf{On-device
classification is treated as a latency or cost problem:} but when cheap
tiers run on-device and the expensive tier is remote, the dominant
quantity is how much content leaves the device, and no published work
reports an \emph{exposure rate} as a tunable curve.

\textbf{Context-aware music systems assume a library:} MusicSense
\cite{musicsense} and comparable recommenders select from a fixed
catalogue, and whether generation beats retrieval on cost is asserted, not
measured. \textbf{Generative audio models do not produce loops:} a
fixed-length excerpt gives no guarantee its end joins its beginning, and
how much a crossfade actually removes is not characterised in the
literature we found.

\subsection{Problem statement}

Given a rendered page $p$, produce continuous ambient audio $a(t)$ perceived
as appropriate to $p$, subject to: \textbf{latency} (audio begins within a
few hundred ms despite a generator taking tens to hundreds of seconds),
\textbf{disclosure} (text leaving the device is minimised, measurable, and
zero for sensitive pages), \textbf{continuity} (a clip repeats without an
audible seam), and \textbf{restraint} (for identifiable page classes, the
correct output is no output, verifiably).

The paper tests whether fallback-then-swap keeps time-to-first-audio
within the latency budget (supported), whether generation covers a
request space retrieval does not (supported), whether loop synthesis
reduces seam discontinuity (qualified, \S\ref{sec:res-loop}), whether
restraint policies fire correctly (negative, \S\ref{sec:res-audit}), and
whether the abstaining cascade reduces exposure at a measurable accuracy
cost (holds only in part, \S\ref{sec:res-classification}).

\section{System Architecture}
\label{sec:arch}
% ===========================================================================

Web2Music is a Manifest V3 Chrome extension plus one HTTP audio-generation
service: four subsystems chained by three versioned handoffs, with the
tiered classifier expanded inline (Fig.~\ref{fig:system}). The division of
labour follows browser security boundaries: the page-descriptor extractor
runs in the content script to reach the DOM, the classifier runs in the
service worker since the content script's origin is untrusted, and
playback/ONNX inference run in an offscreen document since Manifest V3
forbids audio in a service worker and a page's CSP blocks WebAssembly
there. Only the generation service is a network service.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centerline{\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/system.pdf}}
\caption{Process topology, the three handoffs, and the tier cascade inside
the classifier. The middle tier is an entailment model and can therefore
\emph{abstain}, which is what makes its two thresholds a continuous dial
between accuracy and exposure; the remote-LLM tier is the only stage that
transmits page text off the device, and it is unreachable for pages the
sensitive check has already short-circuited to silence/bypass. The dotted
edge carries ONNX inference and IndexedDB access to the offscreen document,
since a page's content security policy blocks both in the page context.}
\label{fig:system}
\end{figure}

\subsection{Page descriptor extraction}

A single function assembles a validated descriptor from six extractors:
text (density-scored boilerplate removal, meta description/language), a
384-dim MiniLM sentence embedding (in-browser ONNX, remote loading
disabled), colour (area-weighted HSL histogram over up to 800 sampled
elements), behaviour (scroll/cursor velocity, throttled to 10/20 events/s),
readability (Flesch \cite{flesch}, unit-normalised), and an IndexedDB
vector store so a revisit reuses a mood (cosine thresholds 0.95 duplicate,
0.85 same-topic, 0.70 related) — vectors from different embedding models
are never compared, since a 384-dim and a 1536-dim vector describe
different spaces, so records are namespaced by backend/model/dimensionality.
URLs are canonicalised, stripping tracking parameters but preserving
meaningful query strings. Extraction stays off the critical path: debounced,
idle-scheduled, re-run on SPA navigation and DOM mutation.

\subsection{The tiered classifier}

The classifier maps the descriptor onto a thirteen-way category and
eleven-way mood, then onto a music profile and prompt (cascade in
Fig.~\ref{fig:system}).
\textbf{Tier 1, keyword heuristic:} a keyword map over title/description/text
produces a category; mood blends the keyword score with an HSL colour bias
and a behaviour bias. At confidence $\geq 0.5$ the pipeline stops here at
essentially zero cost, nothing leaving the device — skipped by construction
for non-English pages, the largest known gap in the design. \textbf{Tier
1.5, zero-shot NLI with abstention:} the cleaned text is scored against
thirteen category hypotheses, returning a label only if the top score is
$\geq 0.45$ \emph{and} margin $\geq 0.10$, otherwise it abstains; two
backends exist, hosted and a local quantised ONNX export, the latter
enabling zero-exposure at some accuracy cost. \textbf{Tier 2, remote LLM:}
a hosted instruction-tuned model, eight-second timeout, fall-back-to-tier-1
on failure — the only tier transmitting page text, whose fraction reaching
it is the exposure rate.

\textbf{Profile, prompt, confidence window.} The committed mood maps to a
music profile (tempo, key, energy, valence, intensity, instruments, timbre,
reverb, ambience, style, dynamics) and thence a prompt; for \emph{focused}:
``lo-fi study, minimal electronic, deep focus. Instruments: piano,
minimalist synth, light percussion, bass drone. Key: D minor, 88 BPM. No
vocals. Seamlessly loopable. Instrumental only.'' A newly classified mood
must remain stable for five seconds before committing — by far the largest
term in the end-to-end wall clock (\S\ref{sec:res-latency}), a design
parameter, not a limitation.

\subsection{Generation, loop synthesis and caching}

The generation service is an HTTP service with a five-stage pipeline
(Table \ref{tab:d-latency}): validate, prompt build, cache check, generate,
post-process/save. Validation fills every missing field with a default, so a
malformed profile degrades rather than fails; the cache is consulted before
the generator, and any stage failure diverts to a pre-generated per-mood
clip rather than an error.

\textbf{Loop synthesis.} The generator produces a one-shot clip, not a
loop. Chroma features feed a self-similarity curve \cite{foote} over
candidate loop lengths $\geq 3$ s; the best candidate snaps to a bar
boundary from beat times \cite{librosa} under a 4/4 assumption, since true
downbeats are unavailable. The clip is truncated there and its head/tail
blended with a 50 ms equal-power crossfade; loudness is normalised to
$-18$ LUFS \cite{ebu128} and exported as Ogg/Opus \cite{opus} rather than
MP3 — not aesthetic, since an MP3 encoder pads the stream audibly on every
loop, so the container, not the crossfade, makes the loop gapless. The
service also computes a diagnostic seam discontinuity (RMS energy and
spectral-centroid differences, before/after crossfade), the objective
measure in \S\ref{sec:res-loop}. \textbf{Cache.} Requests are keyed on
mood/style/key exactly, energy/valence/arousal to one decimal place, a
tempo bucket, a duration bucket, and export codec; the generation seed is
excluded, since retries use fresh seeds and including it would defeat
caching, and a pre-warm grid of 99 combinations is generated ahead of time.

\subsection{Extension shell and playback}

Playback lives in an offscreen document. Two alternating decks over raw
audio buffers honour the detected loop point, feeding four gain stages
(crossfade, user volume, ducking, mood fade) and then an equaliser, reverb
and analyser chain. \textbf{Fallback-then-swap} hides generation latency:
on a committed mood the client requests the fallback clip and a generation
request in parallel; the fallback plays immediately and the generated clip
crossfades in when ready, with one abort controller per request so a new
mood cancels a stale generation rather than overwriting a now-incorrect
track. \textbf{Restraint.} Sensitive pages (crisis, grief, self-harm,
violence) produce silence, short-circuited before either LLM tier; an
audible active tab mutes, a background one ducks; payment, banking,
checkout and browser-internal URLs bypass entirely. \textbf{Telemetry} is
a local-only ring buffer: every URL is SHA-256 hashed before storage and
nothing leaves the machine unless explicitly exported.

\section{Results and Discussion}
\label{sec:results}
% ===========================================================================

\subsection{Evaluation design and provenance}

Five studies: S1 (systems benchmark), S2 (tier ablation), S3 (audio/loop
quality), S4 (within-subjects perceptual), S5 (in-the-wild). S1, the
objective half of S3, the retrieval baseline and restraint audit are
reported here; S4/S5 require ethics approval not yet obtained.
\textbf{Corpus.} 260 pages (255 captured): 182 general, 15
vocabulary-mismatch, 15 non-English, 15 mixed-genre, 12 sensitive, 15
sensitive near-miss, 6 bypass. \textbf{Hardware.} A single CPU laptop; no
GPU configuration was measured.

\subsection{Annotation agreement and human ceiling}
\label{sec:res-agreement}

Three independent annotators labelled all 255 pages for category (13-way),
mood (11-way, a preference not a fact) and sensitivity (binary); Table
\ref{tab:agreement} reports bootstrap Krippendorff's-alpha and
leave-one-annotator-out ceiling for each.

All three label sets fall below the 0.667 minimum for conclusions. At
$\alpha=0.195$, three annotators are close to chance-level agreement on
which of eleven moods a page calls for. Collapsing onto Russell's
valence-arousal circumplex gives $\alpha=0.121$ (system's own mapping) or
$\alpha=0.300$ (NRC lexicon norms \cite{nrcvad}) — the two disagree about
which way coarsening moves agreement, so mood should be reported as a
stylistic preference, not defensible ground truth. Sensitivity diverges
similarly ($\alpha=0.419$ below threshold, ceiling highest of the three at
0.775), consistent with its 87.6\% not-sensitive skew. Any
classifier-accuracy claim here must be read against these ceilings.

\textbf{Post-hoc mood re-collapse (blocked).} A coarser mood vocabulary
(3-way valence/arousal buckets, 2-way positive/negative) would only move
this conclusion if it moved $\alpha$ above 0.667 at some resolution.
Computing that requires the raw per-annotator label files
(\texttt{analysis/out/annotations/*.json}, per \texttt{krippendorff.py}'s
own input contract); those were never committed (\texttt{analysis/out/}
is build output, gitignored) and exist only as the majority-vote labels in
\texttt{s2\_corpus\_annotated.json}, which cannot support a new alpha
computation at a different granularity. This item is blocked on the raw
annotation files existing somewhere outside \texttt{analysis/out/}, not on
missing tooling: \texttt{krippendorff.py}'s \texttt{nominal\_alpha()}
already computes alpha for an arbitrary label set, so re-collapsing is a
same-day job once the three annotators' raw per-page judgements are
available.

\subsection{Which signals earn their cost}
\label{sec:res-signal-ablation}

A per-signal offline ablation (255 pages): category never changes,
decided by text alone. Removing behaviour or the embedding changes mood on
zero pages; removing colour changes it on 7.5 percent, text-alone on 10.2
percent — only colour moves the output measurably, and only for mood. This
ran offline (keyword default, not either model tier), so it bounds rather
than measures colour's contribution live; a live-tier repeat remains future
work (\S\ref{sec:limitations}).

\textbf{Discussion.} Fallback-then-swap makes a 139 s generator feel
immediate, at the cost of the first seconds being generic — nearly
invisible, since a listener cannot miss a clip they never heard. The
dominant wall-clock term is thus the five-second confidence window we
chose, not the model.

\textbf{The 2.08 s cache check was a bug, not a cost, and it is now fixed
(item 50).} \texttt{d5\_cache\_local.py}'s \texttt{check\_cache}/
\texttt{save\_to\_cache}/\texttt{ensure\_schema} opened a brand-new
psycopg2 connection to Postgres and closed it again on every single call,
instead of reusing one. A microbenchmark against a real local Postgres
instance measured $\approx$10 ms/call for connect-query-close and
$\approx$0.1--0.3 ms/call once the same functions are re-run against a
pooled, persistent connection (\texttt{psycopg2.pool.ThreadedConnectionPool},
minconn=1, maxconn=10) — roughly two orders of magnitude, and end-to-end
correctness (MISS $\to$ INSERT $\to$ HIT with the right row) was verified
against a real database, not a mock. The exact 2082 ms figure itself did
not reproduce on this benchmarking host: a fixed $\approx$2 s-per-new-connection
cost is the signature of the well-documented Docker-Desktop-on-Windows
``localhost'' IPv6-then-IPv4 resolution stall, consistent with the
Windows development paths visible elsewhere in the project's run
artefacts, and not a mechanism a native Linux loopback reproduces. Either
way, the fix removes the cause: nothing in \texttt{check\_cache} or
\texttt{save\_to\_cache} opens a new connection per call any more. Table

post-fix re-run of the full D-latency benchmark (item 53's cache-cold
companion) is the remaining step to replace them, and is blocked here on
the same GPU/MusicGen constraints as item 53 (\S\ref{sec:limitations}).

\subsection{Cost and cache behaviour}
\label{sec:res-cost}

Cost accounting over the full S2 corpus supersedes the earlier 18-page
smoke-set figures: the real corpus escalates far more than implied, 75.7
percent of pages reaching the keyword tier's ceiling against 22.2 percent
on the 18-page subset. At the observed rate the cascade sends a mean of
241.5 LLM tokens, 9.84 zero-shot passes, and 1642 bytes per page — 0.0132
USD per thousand pages, rising to 0.0174 USD if every page escalated.
Even the pessimistic number is negligible, so the cascade is worth
building not to save money but because it controls how much browsing
leaves the machine — the disclosure gap this system targets. Audio cost is
dominated by cache behaviour:
expected hit rate is 57 percent under a uniform mood distribution, rising
to 83 percent under heavy skew.

cannot hallucinate a category outside its candidate set.

Fig.~\ref{fig:f5}: Table \ref{tab:tiers} understates the cascade's accuracy

The mechanisms pass; the predicate does not. Against a 47-page adversarial

seven of nineteen false negatives use vocabulary entirely outside the term

This is a negative result for the restraint-policy claim: silence
delivered 34.5 percent of the time is not the policy claimed. The short-circuit fires correctly, but the

the deployment-level gap noted earlier, invisible to any evaluation

\textbf{Correction (item 47a).} The parenthetical above -- ``missed by the
language gate that skips tier 1'' -- does not describe this codebase.
\texttt{runB1} calls \texttt{resolveSensitivity()} unconditionally, before
any language check; there is no gate that skips the sensitivity tier for
non-English text. Reading \texttt{checkSensitiveContent()}/
\texttt{resolveSensitivity()} directly: the four non-English false
negatives happen because \texttt{SEVERE\_SENSITIVE\_TERMS} and
\texttt{AMBIGUOUS\_SENSITIVE\_TERMS} are English-vocabulary term lists, so
a French/German/Portuguese crisis page simply contains none of their
entries -- a vocabulary-coverage gap, not an ordering bug. There is
consequently no config-level fix here (unlike item 47b, \S\ref{sec:res-threshold-sweep});
closing it needs non-English term lists or the entailment tier actually
reaching these pages (item 47, blocked below), not a reordering of
existing checks. The false-negative breakdown above should read: four
non-English (vocabulary-coverage gap, not a gating bug).

\textbf{Item 47 (swap the keyword predicate for the resident entailment
tier, full 47-page re-run) is blocked on network access, not on missing
code.} \texttt{resolveSensitivity()}'s zero-shot promotion/demotion path
already exists and is exactly this fix; \texttt{t5\_audit.mjs} already
supports \texttt{-{}-with-zero-shot} to measure it. Both routes to a live
entailment model -- the hosted \texttt{facebook/bart-large-mnli} proxy
(\texttt{services/classify}, needs \texttt{HF\_API\_TOKEN}) and the local
quantised ONNX checkpoint (\texttt{@xenova/transformers}, needs a
one-time download from huggingface.co) -- require network access this
evaluation environment does not have and no \texttt{.env} with the
relevant tokens configured. This is the same blocker as item 52
(\S\ref{sec:limitations}): both need the identical entailment model, one
over the 47-page adversarial slice, the other over the full 254-page S2
corpus.

% ===========================================================================
\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
% ===========================================================================

We presented Web2Music, a browser extension conditioning generative ambient
music on the page being read: a four-signal page descriptor costing 86 ms at
the median, of which only colour moves either output measurably; a
three-tier abstaining classifier that classifies a thousand pages for about
1.3 cents, though its thresholds redistribute disclosure rather than
reducing total exposure; a fallback-then-swap design hiding a 139 s
generator behind a 7 ms placeholder; and loop synthesis closing a one-shot
output into a gapless loop. Four results run against the design's own
assumptions: the crossfade removes a median of only 0.93 dB of seam
discontinuity; half the generated audio is discarded to find a loop point;
the sensitive-content detector misses 65.5 percent of an adversarial crisis
slice; and three annotators agree on the system's mood label at only
$\alpha=0.195$, rising to just $0.300$ under Russell's valence-arousal
quadrants (\S\ref{sec:res-agreement}). The central perceptual claim, that
page-conditioned music fits the page better than page-agnostic music,
remains untested.

% ===========================================================================
\section{Limitations and Future Work}
\label{sec:limitations}
% ===========================================================================

The central perceptual claim is untested: everything here is about the
system, not its effect on people, and the immediate priority is
institutional ethics approval, followed by the within-subjects study and
the in-the-wild deployment. Mood has no useful inter-annotator ceiling
(\S\ref{sec:res-agreement}: 0.174, below the raw eleven-way agreement
itself), unlike category (0.621) and sensitivity (0.775); a post-hoc
re-collapse at coarser mood granularity remains blocked on the raw
per-annotator label files not being committed anywhere in the repository
(\S\ref{sec:res-agreement}). Table
\ref{tab:tiers}'s tier-ablation numbers are a conservative floor:
re-scored against the three-annotator label, the full cascade's accuracy
cost relative to keyword-to-LLM widens to an estimated 7.7 points, 95\%
interval [2.9, 12.5], and the local backend that could reduce total
disclosure rather than redistribute it has only been measured on an
18-page subset, even though the real corpus escalates at 75.7 percent, not
the smoke set's 22.2 — the highest-value item remaining, and currently
blocked in this evaluation environment on network access to the
HuggingFace-hosted checkpoint it requires (items 47 and 52 alike). The
sensitive-content predicate needs replacing with a model-based detector,
plausibly the entailment tier already resident in the extension, since the
65.5 percent false-negative rate on that policy is a defect to fix before
any deployment, not a limitation to note in passing; the keyword tier is
also skipped by construction on non-English pages for category, and the
sensitive detector's own non-English misses are a term-list vocabulary gap
rather than a shared gating bug (\S\ref{sec:res-audit}). Every
latency figure comes from one CPU laptop, with a GPU configuration and a
cache-cold comparison both unmeasured — the cold-cache re-run (item 53) is
the most overdue gap, since no generation-latency figure here reflects
live cache-miss generation, and it remains blocked in this evaluation
environment: MusicGen inference needs a model download this network
cannot reach and is CPU-bound at $\sim$139 s/clip, so $n\geq30$ cold
generations is a multi-hour run better suited to the original development
machine than a sandboxed re-run. For loop synthesis, the crossfade-window
sweep and seam-minimising selection proposed here warrant a production
change pending confirmation at the full 141-clip scale, and the larger
opportunity is conditioning the generator to produce loop-closed material
directly; both remain blocked at that scale because the pre-truncation
source audio for the 141 generated clips was never retained
(\S\ref{sec:res-loop}), which future data-collection passes should treat
as a requirement, not an afterthought, given how much of \S\ref{sec:res-loop}
had to fall back to the 10-clip fallback sample as a result. Finally, the
language gate deserves dedicated treatment, and the
same descriptor-to-profile machinery would apply to other continuous
contexts — documents, code editors, reading applications.
